# Direction

Purpose: give collaborators an immediate sense of Raphael’s work across music, education, and automation, then let them explore concrete projects and get in touch.

Tone: a playful physical workshop, with warm paper, oversized ink typography, cobalt hardware, and small, tactile moments of discovery.

Constraint: buildless HTML, CSS, and JavaScript; local assets and fonts; responsive layouts; keyboard-operable controls; native dialogs; reduced motion and a persistent-in-session motion toggle. No fabricated performance or customer claims.

Differentiator: the curiosity machine. A large tactile dial introduces the relationship between deliberate engineering and play, then real sound and an actual miniature game carry that idea into the work.

Content: a poster-like personal introduction, five selected projects, two smaller experiments, a human introduction, a three-step process, and an email invitation.

Interaction: a staggered entrance for the machine, restrained scroll reveals, tactile project hovers, an optional Rahu audio preview, a curiosity dial with a small surprise, and a user-initiated stacking game. Continuous decorative motion can be paused; operating-system preferences are respected.

## People-first revision — 28 October 2026

Purpose: help people in social initiatives recognise their own needs and understand how Raphael can work alongside them.
Tone: the same warm, playful character, now centred on conversation and shared learning.
Differentiation: a small group of friendly illustrated people replaces the hardware dial. The curiosity control stays a secondary invitation to play.
Content order: people-first introduction → working together → meet Raphael → examples (education and coordination first) → collaborative process → conversation. Sound and code remain evidence and creative interests, rather than the main identity.
Constraints: preserve the existing projects and game, keyboard accessibility, reduced motion, and static hosting. Do not imply unverified client outcomes.
